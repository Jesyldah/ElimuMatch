## One-Page Insert (for main capstone document)

### Data Sources

This project delivers a **proof of concept** for student retention risk scoring and support matching in Kenyan secondary education. Because partner student records were not available during development (privacy, access timelines, and data-sharing constraints), analysis uses a **fully synthetic cohort** of 1,000 students across 20 schools (`elimu_match_data_v4.csv`), generated by a reproducible script (`synthetic_data_v2.py`, seed: 2026). The dataset is **not** presented as empirical evidence from a live population; it demonstrates an end-to-end analytics pipeline ready for real data ingestion.

### Cohort Design

The synthetic Data Generating Process (DGP) encodes domain-informed structure: SES-weighted economic quintiles, conditional access barriers (digital equity, commute, school feeding, counseling), linked academic variables (GPA trend ↔ failed subjects), health-driven absences, realistic missingness (5–10% on survey fields), and a binary outcome (`retained`, ~86% cohort retention). A post-outcome field (`dropout_reason`) supports intervention routing for non-retained students but is excluded from predictive modeling.

### Modeling & Controls

The modeling pipeline (`train_retention_model.py`) excludes leakage columns (`retention_risk_score`, `dropout_reason`, `academic_catchup_status`), imputes missing values via median imputation, and evaluates logistic regression and random forest models (test AUC ~0.75). Class weighting prioritizes recall for at-risk students. Performance is reported overall and by SES quintile to assess equity of model utility.

### Limitations

Findings reflect **assumptions encoded in synthetic data**, not validated real-world performance. The model has not been tested on partner administrative records. Support-program variables are partially correlated with SES; imputation is simplified; and unobserved shocks (fee crises, relocation, school closure) are absent. Risk scores are intended for **support allocation**, not exclusionary decisions.

### Next Steps

(1) Secure partner data under MOU/ethics review; (2) map real records to the PoC schema; (3) retrain and recalibrate on live data; (4) conduct fairness review by subgroup; (5) pilot intervention routing and measure retention lift.

**Integrity statement:** All performance metrics and dropout patterns cited in this capstone are PoC outputs unless explicitly validated on partner data.

## Full Documentation (expanded reference)

**Analyst note:** The sections below expand on the one-page insert for appendix or reviewer reference.


### 1. Data Sources & Scope

This capstone develops a **proof of concept (PoC)** for identifying students at elevated dropout risk and routing them to appropriate support interventions (financial aid, academic remediation, health services, commute assistance, and counseling). The analytics pipeline is designed for Kenyan secondary-school contexts, where student-level administrative data is often fragmented, privacy-sensitive, or unavailable during early partnership stages.

**Primary dataset:** `elimu_match_data_v4.csv` — a **fully synthetic cohort** of **1,000 students** across **20 schools**, generated by a documented Python script (`synthetic_data_v2.py`, reproducible seed: 2026).

**Why synthetic data was used:** Real student records were not available for this PoC due to anticipated constraints around data-sharing agreements, student privacy, and collection timelines. Synthetic data allows the team to demonstrate an end-to-end analytics workflow—ingestion, imputation, modeling, evaluation, and intervention logic—without exposing identifiable minors or misrepresenting proprietary partner data.

**Supporting artifacts:**
- `synthetic_data_v2.py` — data generation logic (Data Generating Process, or DGP)
- `train_retention_model.py` — modeling pipeline with leakage controls and fairness-oriented evaluation

---

### 2. Synthetic Cohort Design

The synthetic generator was built to reflect **domain-informed structure**, not random noise. Key design choices include:

| Design element | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **SES-weighted quintiles** | Lower/middle economic strata are over-represented, matching typical secondary-school populations in resource-constrained settings. |
| **Conditional disadvantage** | Socioeconomic status influences digital access, commute distance, nutritional program coverage, counseling access, and household cash-flow volatility. |
| **Academic linkage** | GPA trend is inversely related to failed subject count; science aptitude is partially tied to academic performance. |
| **Health–attendance link** | Chronic health risk drives illness-related absences. |
| **Missing values (5–10%)** | Introduced on harder-to-collect survey fields (`cash_flow_volatility`, `commute_barrier_score`, `digital_equity_access_score`, `psychosocial_support_access`), with slightly higher missingness among lower-SES students (missing at random / missing not at random hybrid). |
| **Outcome: `retained`** | Binary retention indicator; cohort retention rate calibrated to **~86%**, with lower retention in bottom SES quintiles. |
| **Post-outcome: `dropout_reason`** | Assigned only for non-retained students (e.g., academic, financial, health, commute) to support intervention targeting—not used as a model input. |

The DGP is **transparent and reproducible**. Any stakeholder or reviewer can regenerate the dataset and audit assumptions directly from code.

---

### 3. Variables & Outcome Definition

**Target variable:** `retained` (1 = student remained enrolled; 0 = dropped out).

**Feature domains (16 predictors in the modeling pipeline):**
- Demographics & household structure (age, gender, household size proxy)
- Socioeconomic status index (quintile 1–5)
- Access barriers (commute, digital equity, school feeding, counseling)
- Academic performance (GPA trend, failed subjects, science aptitude)
- Health & attendance (chronic risk score, illness absences)
- Social integration & economic volatility

**Excluded from training (leakage / redundancy controls):**
- `retention_risk_score` — latent oracle probability used only during data generation
- `dropout_reason` — assigned after the outcome is known
- `academic_catchup_status` — largely deterministic from failed subject count
- `student_id` — identifier only

Missing survey fields are imputed via **median imputation** in the PoC pipeline; production deployment would require a documented imputation policy validated on real data.

### 4. Modeling Results (PoC — Qualified)

Using a stratified 75/25 train–test split (`train_retention_model.py`):

| Model | Test AUC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Logistic Regression (class-weighted) | ~0.75 | Higher recall on at-risk (dropped) students |
| Random Forest (class-weighted) | ~0.75 | Higher overall accuracy; lower dropout recall |

**Interpretation guardrail:** These metrics demonstrate that the pipeline extracts **structurally meaningful signal** from the documented synthetic cohort. They do **not** constitute validation of predictive performance on real Kenyan student populations. AUC is strongest for lower SES quintiles where retention variance is greatest; upper quintiles show weaker rank-ordering because nearly all students are retained.

---

### 5. Limitations

1. **Synthetic-only evidence base.** All quantitative findings reflect assumptions encoded in the DGP. Relationships are cleaner than in administrative or survey data and may overstate model separability.

2. **No external validation.** The model has not been tested against held-out real records, ministry data, or a partner school's historical cohort.

3. **Simplified causal structure.** Retention is modeled as a function of observable features plus noise. Real dropout is influenced by shocks (fee crises, family relocation, school closures) not captured here.

4. **Support-program endogeneity.** Nutritional and psychosocial support are partially correlated with SES in the DGP; a model may attribute effects to demographics that are proxied through program access.

5. **Imputation simplicity.** Median imputation is adequate for PoC demonstration but may bias variance and uncertainty estimates in production.

6. **Generalizability.** Results apply to the synthetic cohort's parameterization (age 13–17, 20 schools, ~1,000 students) and may not transfer across regions, school types, or policy environments.

7. **Ethical deployment.** Risk scores must not be used for exclusionary decisions (e.g., denying enrollment). The intended use is **support allocation**, not student sorting or punishment.

---

### 6. Validation Roadmap & Next Steps

To move from PoC to production-ready analytics, the following steps are recommended:

1. **Establish a data-sharing framework** with a partner school network or NGO (MOU, consent protocols, IRB/ethics review if required).
2. **Ingest real administrative records** (enrollment, attendance, grades, fee status) and map them to the PoC schema.
3. **Retrain and recalibrate** models on real data; compare AUC, calibration, and recall for the lowest SES quintile.
4. **Conduct fairness review** — ensure error rates do not systematically harm vulnerable subgroups (gender, region, disability status when available).
5. **Pilot intervention routing** — match high-risk students to support categories aligned with `dropout_reason` logic; measure retention lift in a controlled rollout.
6. **Replace median imputation** with model-based or domain-informed imputation, and quantify sensitivity of predictions to missing data.

---

### 7. Statement of Analytic Integrity

All results in this capstone that reference model performance, retention rates, or dropout drivers should be cited as **proof-of-concept outputs from a documented synthetic cohort**, unless and until replicated on partner data. The value of this submission lies in the **consultancy framework, reproducible pipeline, and intervention design**—not in empirical claims about a specific live student population.
